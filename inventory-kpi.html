Inventory analytics · Builds on my LG work

Inventory KPI Builder: from stock movements to a reorder list.

At LG, the barcode system recorded every item taken from or returned to a supply room. This tool takes that kind of log and turns it into what a coordinator needs: current balances, how many days each item will last, what to reorder, what has run out before, and which records look wrong.

The demo uses synthetic data I generated to resemble a supply-room scan log (39 consumable items, 3 rooms, about four months). It is not LG data. Uploaded files stay in your browser.

Expected columns

Required: date or timestamp, item ID, action, quantity. Optional: item name, supply room, unit cost, minimum level.
Actions: ISSUE (checkout), RECEIVE (check-in or delivery), OPENING (starting balance), ADJUST (signed count correction). Common synonyms such as OUT, IN, CHECKOUT and RESTOCK are recognized.

How the numbers are calculated

  • On hand is OPENING + RECEIVE + ADJUST − ISSUE for every record up to the last date in the file. Records with an unreadable date, item, action or quantity are excluded and counted under Data checks.
  • Days of supply is on hand divided by average daily issues over the chosen window. Items with no issues in the window are shown as No movement.
  • Status. Out of stock: on hand at or below zero. Reorder now: at or below the minimum, or less than one lead time of supply. Watch: less than two lead times of supply.
  • Suggested order brings stock up to the larger of the minimum and the target cover days at the current usage rate.
  • Stockout episodes count the times an item’s running balance reached zero or below during the whole file.
  • Annualized turnover is issued value in the window, scaled to 365 days, divided by current inventory value. It is shown only when unit costs are present.
  • ABC classes rank items by issued value in the window (issued units if no costs): A is the top 80% of value, B the next 15%, C the rest.
  • Usage rates come from past issues only. They do not forecast demand changes, and a short window can overstate a one-off spike.
