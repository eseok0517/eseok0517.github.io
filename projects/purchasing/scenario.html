02 / Management perspective

A discount can be profitable and still consume more cash.

01

Separate cash from savings

Inventory reduction releases capital. Carrying-cost reductions are period savings. Adding both into a single annual “savings” figure would overstate the result.

02

Price the service tradeoff

More frequent ordering reduces average inventory but exposes more demand to replenishment delays. Review item-level shortages before changing critical consumables.

03

Make assumptions visible

The supplier discount and carrying rate are assumptions. Adjust them above to see when a low-cost purchasing policy changes.

03 / Evidence & method

From messy exports to a purchasing decision.

Data preparation

2,916 raw purchase records contain 36 duplicated transaction IDs and inconsistent identifier spacing. The pipeline removes exact duplicates, trims IDs, converts packs to pieces and reconciles line spending.

Raw export ↓ · Clean export ↓

Holdout design

Jan 2024–Jun 2025 calibrates average demand and an uncertainty buffer. Jul–Dec 2025 is held out. Current, frequent and bulk policies are fixed at 90, 30 and 120 days of training demand.

Simulation source ↗

Financial definitions

Carrying cost = average inventory × annual rate × 180/360. Freight is $45 per order. Lost units incur an assumed penalty of 50% of standard unit cost. Bulk orders receive a 4% discount by default.

Read the management memo ↗

Limitations

All company data is synthetic. Months have 30 days; lead times are fixed; shortages are lost demand. Opening inventory differs by policy and includes no transition cost. Results compare steady policies, not a cash-flow forecast for switching today.
