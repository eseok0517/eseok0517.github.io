A supply-room system that keeps counts and reorder alerts current from scan logs

I built this to extend my LG internship work. It keeps what worked, barcode check-in and check-out with running counts, and adds usage-based reorder points, suggested order quantities and live updates across every room.

01

The problem

At LG, each supply room kept its own Excel workbook and macros. Counts were updated room by room, reorder levels were minimums someone typed in, and combining the rooms into one report took most of the effort.

The aim was one tool that keeps counts and reorder alerts current directly from scan logs, and that anyone can open and try without installing or enabling macros.

02

The data

The demo runs on scan logs I generated to resemble a real supply-room log: three rooms, 48 consumable items and about 90 days of check-ins and check-outs. LG records are confidential and are not used.

The tool also accepts any CSV movement log. Columns are matched by name, so exact headers are not required, and the log export uses the same columns as the original workbooks.

03

What I built

  1. Record movements. Scan or type an item, confirm the quantity, and the log updates. Entries are checked for unknown items, wrong unit types and quantities above stock.
  2. Compute balances. On hand is opening stock plus receipts and adjustments minus issues, updated after every scan.
  3. Set reorder points from usage. Average daily use over the last 90 days times supplier lead time gives each item a reorder point, days of supply and a suggested order quantity, replacing hand-set minimums.
  4. Report KPIs. Inventory value, usage, days of supply and ABC classes, with CSV export in the same columns the workbook logs used. A companion CSV analyzer adds stockout history and turnover for any movement log.

04

What I found

  • Reorder points now follow measured usage. In the Excel version each minimum was typed in and went stale. Here every item gets a reorder point, days of supply and a suggested order automatically from its own history.
  • Consolidation disappears. One app updates every room live, so the manual step of merging per-room workbooks into a master file is no longer needed.
  • The workflow it builds on worked. The LG version tracked 300+ items across three supply rooms and cut bi-weekly reporting time by about 67%. The web version keeps that workflow and removes its manual steps.

05

What I would do next

Run it on an anonymized log from a real supply room, and measure how often its reorder alerts match what coordinators would have ordered. Then add a shared database so several people can scan at once, and a demand forecast that handles seasonal items.
