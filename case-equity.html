How well-known stocks and ETFs performed across market regimes

Analyzed ten widely held stocks and ETFs against yield-curve and inflation data. Reported risk-adjusted return, drawdowns and correlations, showed performance under different macro conditions, and included a portfolio builder.

01

The problem

Investors compare stocks by return, but return alone hides risk. The question was how ten well-known holdings differ once risk and drawdowns are counted, and whether the macro environment, specifically an inverted yield curve or high inflation, changes which holdings do well.

02

The data

Month-end adjusted prices for SPY, QQQ, AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, JPM, GS, XOM, JNJ and TLT, from a public price source with a second source as a fallback. The 10-year minus 2-year Treasury spread, CPI and the 3-month Treasury bill rate come from FRED. A GitHub Action refreshes both every week.

03

What I built

  1. Measure risk and return. Annualized return, volatility, Sharpe ratio, beta and correlation against SPY, and worst drawdown for each holding.
  2. Label each month by regime. The yield curve is inverted when the 10-year spread is below zero; inflation is high when CPI is up 3% or more from a year earlier.
  3. Average what came next. For each regime, the average return over the following 12 months, with the number of independent episodes stated.
  4. Build portfolios. Pick holdings and weights, rebalance monthly, and compare with SPY.
